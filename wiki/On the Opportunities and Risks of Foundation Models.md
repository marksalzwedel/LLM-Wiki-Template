# On the Opportunities and Risks of Foundation Models

## Summary

This report names and analyzes [[Foundation Models]]: models trained on broad data, usually with [[Self-Supervised Learning]] at scale, that can be adapted to many downstream tasks.

Source file: [raw/2108.07258v3.pdf](../raw/2108.07258v3.pdf)

## Explanation

The report reframes models like [[BERT]], [[GPT-3]], and DALL-E as part of a shared paradigm. It emphasizes [[Emergence]], [[Homogenization]], [[Adaptation]], [[Evaluation]], [[Bias and Fairness]], [[Misuse]], [[Environmental Impact]], security, privacy, legality, economics, and [[Alignment]]. Its key contribution here is not a new model, but a sociotechnical map of why general-purpose models are high leverage and high risk.

## Contradictions and Tensions

- Earlier papers largely measure progress through task performance. This report argues that downstream reuse can also amplify shared defects.
- It complicates the optimism of [[Scaling Language Models]] by emphasizing that emergent capabilities are not fully predictable or understood.
- It agrees that models such as [[BERT]] and [[GPT-3]] are powerful, but treats that power as a reason to broaden evaluation beyond standard NLP benchmarks.
